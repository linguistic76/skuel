## Emotions Are Signals

An emotion is information. It tells you something about how you're relating to what's happening. Anger says a boundary was crossed. Anxiety says something feels uncertain. Joy says something matters to you. Sadness says something was lost.

None of these are problems. They're signals — like the dashboard lights in a car. You don't rip out the check engine light. You read it.

Most people learn to do one of two things with emotions: act on them immediately, or shove them down. Self-reflection offers a third option: **feel them, observe them, and then choose what to do.**

## Beyond "Good" and "Bad"

When someone asks how you feel, you probably answer with one of five words: good, bad, fine, stressed, tired. These are emotional compression — they flatten a complex signal into a vague summary.

Real emotional awareness means expanding the vocabulary:

Instead of "bad," what is it really?
- Frustrated — something isn't working the way you expected
- Disappointed — an expectation wasn't met
- Embarrassed — you feel exposed
- Lonely — you feel disconnected
- Overwhelmed — too much is coming at once
- Resentful — you feel treated unfairly

Each of these points to something different. "Bad" tells you nothing useful. "Resentful" tells you exactly where to look.

The same applies to "good":
- Grateful — something meaningful was given or shared
- Proud — you lived up to your own standards
- Curious — something sparked your interest
- Relieved — a pressure lifted
- Connected — you feel seen by someone

Precision isn't about being dramatic. It's about being accurate. The more precisely you can name what you feel, the more clearly you can understand what's driving your behavior.

## The 30-Second Sit

This is the core practice. When an emotion shows up — any emotion, pleasant or unpleasant:

1. **Notice it.** "I'm feeling something."
2. **Name it.** As precisely as you can. "I'm feeling resentful."
3. **Locate it.** Where is it in your body? Tight chest? Hot face? Heavy stomach? Clenched jaw?
4. **Sit with it for 30 seconds.** Don't act on it. Don't push it away. Don't analyze why it's there. Just let it be present.
5. **Notice what happens.** Emotions are not static. They shift, soften, intensify, morph into other feelings. Watching this happen in real time is profoundly informative.

This is not suppression. Suppression says "I shouldn't feel this." Observation says "I do feel this. Let me watch it." The difference matters enormously. Suppressed emotions leak out sideways. Observed emotions teach you about yourself.

## Emotions in the Body

Emotions aren't just mental events. They show up physically — often before you're consciously aware of the feeling:

- **Anger/frustration:** Heat in the face and chest, clenched fists, tight jaw
- **Anxiety:** Tight stomach, shallow breathing, restless legs
- **Sadness:** Heavy chest, pressure behind the eyes, low energy
- **Joy:** Open chest, light feeling, spontaneous smile
- **Shame:** Desire to shrink, heat in the face, looking down

Learning to read your body is a shortcut to emotional awareness. Sometimes you'll notice the physical sensation before you can name the emotion — and the body signal will help you find the word.

## Practice: The Evening Check-In

At the end of today, take two minutes to answer three questions:

1. **What was the strongest emotion I felt today?** Name it precisely.
2. **What triggered it?** What happened right before?
3. **Where did I feel it in my body?** Be specific.

Write it down — even just three short lines in a notebook or your phone. Over a week of these check-ins, patterns will emerge that you never saw before.

## Values Are Not Aspirations

Most "find your values" exercises hand you a list of words — honesty, creativity, family, success — and ask you to pick your top five. That's not values discovery. That's a vocabulary quiz. Everyone picks "honesty." Almost no one reflects on whether they actually prioritize it when it costs them something.

Real values aren't chosen from a list. They're discovered by looking at how you've actually lived. Your values are whatever you consistently prioritize when things get hard — not what you think you *should* prioritize.

## The Evidence Is in Your Choices

Your values reveal themselves in decisions, especially difficult ones:

- You turned down a higher-paying job to stay close to family → **Family / Belonging**
- You stayed up late to finish a project no one would have noticed was incomplete → **Craftsmanship / Integrity**
- You spoke up in a meeting when everyone else stayed quiet → **Courage / Honesty**
- You chose the harder class because you wanted to actually learn → **Growth / Mastery**
- You gave time to someone who needed help even when you were busy → **Generosity / Connection**

These aren't hypothetical scenarios. Think about your own life. When you made a hard choice, what did you choose? The answer reveals what you value.

## The Evidence Is in Your Emotions

Strong emotions are value signals:

- **Anger** often signals a value being violated. What made you angry recently? What value was being stepped on?
- **Pride** signals a value being honored. What are you genuinely proud of? What value does that achievement reflect?
- **Guilt** signals a value you betrayed. What do you feel guilty about? What value were you not living up to?
- **Admiration** signals a value you aspire to. Who do you admire? What quality in them speaks to you?

If you did the emotional awareness practices, you already have data. Look back at your check-ins. The strongest emotions point to the deepest values.

## Inherited vs Held

Some values are genuinely yours. Others were installed by parents, culture, peers, or media. The difference matters.

An inherited value sounds like: "I should value X because good people value X."
A held value sounds like: "I can't *not* care about X. It just matters to me."

The test: imagine no one would ever know what you chose. Would you still choose the same way? If yes, the value is held. If the choice changes when the audience disappears, the value is inherited.

Both kinds are real. But self-reflection means knowing which is which. You might discover that some values you thought were yours are actually borrowed — and some you never articulated are the ones actually driving your life.

## Practice: Three Moments

Think of three moments from the past year:

1. **A moment you felt proud.** What did you do? What value does it reflect?
2. **A moment you felt angry or frustrated.** What was violated? What value was being stepped on?
3. **A moment you made a hard choice.** What did you choose, and what did you give up? What does the choice reveal?

Write down the value each moment points to. Don't use fancy words — plain language is better. "Being honest even when it's awkward" is more useful than "integrity."

If two or three of your moments point to the same value, pay attention. That's a core value — not because you picked it from a list, but because your life has been organized around it whether you knew it or not.

## What Values Are For

Values aren't decorations. They're decision tools. When you know what you value:

- You can say no to things that don't align, without guilt
- You can say yes to hard things that do align, with confidence
- You can understand *why* certain situations drain you (value conflict) and others energize you (value expression)
- You can stop measuring yourself by someone else's standards and start measuring by your own

Self-reflection without values is just observation. Values give the observation meaning. "I noticed I avoid conflict" is data. "I avoid conflict because I value harmony — and sometimes I sacrifice honesty to keep the peace" is self-knowledge.

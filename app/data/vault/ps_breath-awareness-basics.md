## Why Breath?

You need an anchor — something to direct your attention toward. Breath works best for beginners because:

- **It's always there.** You don't need equipment, an app, or a quiet room.
- **It's rhythmic.** The rise and fall gives attention something to follow.
- **It's neutral.** There's no right or wrong way to breathe. You're just noticing what's already happening.

Breath is not the point of mindfulness. It's the training tool. The point is building the capacity to *notice* — and once you can notice your breath, you can notice your thoughts, emotions, reactions, and patterns.

## The Two-Minute Practice

Here's the whole thing. Two minutes.

1. **Sit comfortably.** Chair, floor, cushion — doesn't matter. Spine naturally upright, not rigid. Shoulders relaxed. Hands wherever they're comfortable. If you want to close your eyes, close them. If not, soften your gaze toward the floor.

2. **Find the breath.** Don't change it. Just notice where you feel it most — the cool air at your nostrils, the rise and fall of your chest, the expansion of your belly. Pick one spot and stay there.

3. **Follow it.** Inhale... notice. Exhale... notice. That's the whole practice. You're not counting, not controlling, not trying to breathe a certain way. Just watching.

4. **When your mind wanders — and it will — come back.** Gently. No frustration, no self-criticism. Just: "Oh, I was thinking about dinner. Back to the breath."

5. **After two minutes, stop.** Open your eyes. Notice how you feel. Done.

Two minutes. That's the session. You can do it right now before reading the next section.

## Observation vs Control

The most common beginner mistake is trying to *control* the breath instead of *watching* it. You'll catch yourself deepening your inhale or slowing your exhale because you think that's what you're supposed to do.

You're not. The breath is doing its own thing. Your job is to watch it — like sitting by a river and watching the water flow. You don't push the water. You just notice it.

When you catch yourself controlling, that noticing is itself a moment of awareness. Smile at it. Let the breath return to its own rhythm. You're learning.

## Practice: Find Your Spot

Right now, take three natural breaths and answer one question: where do you feel the breath most?

- **Nostrils?** The cool air entering, warm air leaving.
- **Chest?** The rise and fall of the ribcage.
- **Belly?** The expansion and contraction below the ribs.

There's no correct answer. Wherever the sensation is clearest for you — that's your anchor point. Remember it. You'll use it every time you sit.

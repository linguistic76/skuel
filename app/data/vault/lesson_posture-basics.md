## Posture Supports the Practice

Posture is not the practice. It supports the practice — like a good chair supports reading. Get it roughly right and forget about it.

The one principle: **alert but relaxed.** If you're rigid, you'll create tension. If you're slouching, you'll drift toward sleep. The sweet spot is somewhere between — upright enough to stay awake, soft enough to stay comfortable.

## The Setup

- **Spine:** Naturally upright, not military-straight. Imagine a string gently pulling from the crown of your head. Your spine has curves — let them be there.
- **Shoulders:** Drop them. Away from your ears. Most people carry tension here without noticing. Roll them back once, then let them settle.
- **Hands:** Wherever they're comfortable. Lap, knees, armrests. There's no secret hand position that makes meditation work better.
- **Head:** Balanced on your neck, chin slightly tucked. Not looking up, not looking down. Neutral.
- **Eyes:** Closed, or softly gazing at a spot on the floor about two feet ahead. Either works.

## Four Positions

**Chair.** Feet flat on the floor. Sit away from the backrest so your spine supports itself. This is the most practical position for most people.

**Cushion.** Cross-legged on the floor, hips elevated on a cushion or folded blanket. Hips higher than knees reduces strain. If your knees float high above the ground, use a higher cushion or switch to a chair.

**Bench.** Kneeling with a small bench or cushion between your calves. Takes pressure off the knees while keeping a natural spinal curve.

**Lying down.** On your back, arms at your sides. Perfectly valid — especially if sitting causes pain. The trade-off: you might fall asleep. If that happens, it's not a disaster. Your body needed rest. But if it happens every time, try sitting instead.

## Don't Let Posture Be a Barrier

The biggest posture mistake isn't having the wrong position — it's letting posture concerns stop you from practicing.

Your knees hurt on the floor? Sit in a chair. Can't sit? Stand. Can't stand? Lie down. The position matters far less than the fact that you showed up.

Perfection is not the goal. Two minutes in an imperfect position beats zero minutes in a theoretically perfect one.

## Practice: The Body Scan Check

Before your next breath session, take 10 seconds to scan:

1. **Shoulders** — Are they creeping up? Drop them.
2. **Jaw** — Is it clenched? Soften it.
3. **Hands** — Are they gripping? Let them rest open.

Three checkpoints. Ten seconds. Then start your breath practice. Over time this scan becomes automatic — you'll notice tension and release it without thinking about it.
